Game Physics & Feel.

PLAYABLE PROTOTYPE / IN DEVELOPMENT

A 2D puzzle platformer exploring how equations, frame-by-frame simulation, and small control rules become a player's experience of movement.

TypeScriptReactHTML CanvasCustom movement & collision

What good game feel means to me

For me, good game feel means that the character responds in the way I expect. A jump does not have to follow real-world physics exactly to feel right. What matters is whether the game understands what I am trying to do.

Celeste made me interested in the small rules behind that feeling. Coyote time gives a player a brief chance to jump after leaving an edge. Input buffering remembers a jump pressed just before landing, while corner correction can help a character clear a slightly misjudged corner. These adjustments make me think about the difference between following an input literally and responding to the intention behind it.

I also want movement to feel clear and satisfying. Animation, sound, a short freeze, or a dash trail can help communicate the force of an action. I want to explore how much feedback is enough to make movement expressive without getting in the player's way.

Finally, a failed attempt should leave me wanting to try again. Quick restarts keep the previous attempt fresh in my mind, so I can adjust and learn. With this project, I hope to bring these ideas together: responsive controls, thoughtful forgiveness, and challenges that feel worth another attempt.

Movement & control

The controller is written directly in the project,
rather than delegated to a rigid-body physics engine.

01 / MOVEMENT

Direction & response

Variable-height jumps, different ground and air responses, and eight-direction dashes. Direction vectors are normalized so diagonal dashes do not start faster.

02 / GEOMETRY

Contact & correction

Axis-aligned rectangles detect overlap. Movement is resolved one axis at a time, with one-way platforms and some corner correction.

03 / INPUT

A little room for error

A brief window after leaving an edge and a buffered jump before landing let the rules accommodate slightly early or late inputs.

Signal Forge + live debugger

A level editor supports room layouts, entity properties, undo/redo, JSON import/export, and playtesting. A debugger exposes position, velocity, movement state, and collision bounds.

In progress

Current scope

The prototype combines movement, puzzle elements, an editor, and debugging tools. It is an ongoing learning project, with no claim of a completed player study or a new collision algorithm.

Open questions

Frame-rate consistency, high-speed contact with thin obstacles, and repeatable physics tests remain areas to investigate. Triangle-based slope collision is planned.
